# Surface brief: prototype-paper (owner-approved light redesign, step 1)

Scope: one standalone file holding every page in Arabic and English, for the owner's review. Mode: Persuade.
Audience and job: procurement officers and project engineers checking qualification; private developers sourcing a subcontractor. Action: the single contact action «تواصل معنا».
Constraints: the owner's brief in prototype-paper/BRIEF.md is binding (ten-colour palette, moderate type sizes, one action, no repetition, compact footer, real content only). After seeing the first build the owner asked for a formal Arabic face (naskh or Almarai) and for the look to match the reference site's own professionalism: serif headings, light header, pale cards. Menu, filter and tabs must work with JavaScript off.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A contractor's qualification file laid out on warm paper: one fact-set per section, with wide empty margins, and photographs doing the persuading. It refuses the icon-card grid, the repeated call-to-action and the logo wall.

OWN-WORLD: Paper ground #F7F6F3 with slate ink #364044. Headings in a light serif with naskh for Arabic (Instrument Serif, Noto Naskh Arabic), set in two tones (ink, then muted #7B7C79) under a small section label; body and interface in a plain sans (Onest, Almarai). Pale stone plates (a tint of #C9C8C3) with a hairline foot. One full-bleed warm stone band #A99E8F and one soft teal close #839FA3. Wide photograph cards with the title set on the photograph, and large rounded photograph panels with paper cards floating on them. Numbered hairline rows. One thin-line isometric drawing of layered infrastructure. A paper header carrying the circular mark and the company's name as text; the official lockup, whose wordmark is white, sits on the slate footer.

STORY: The visitor learns who the company is (name over a real site photograph, four figures), what it delivers (nine disciplines), what it has built and for whom (six projects with client names), who approves it (seven bodies, three ISO standards), who leads it, and where to reach it.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The site photograph fills the screen under a slate veil. On wide screens the header lies over it: mark and name at the start, five links, the language switch. On phones the header is a paper bar above the photograph. Centred: the company name as h1 in the display face, the lead sentence, and the one contact action as a paper button with a small slate icon inside a translucent frame. Along the bottom edge: the four figures in one row (two rows on phones).

SIGNATURE: the paper page slides up over the fixed photograph; the project filter narrows six photograph cards in place.
